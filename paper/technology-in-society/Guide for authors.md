## About the Journal

### Aims and Scope

*Technology in Society* is an international journal devoted to the global discourse on the dynamics of technological change and the intersection of social, economic, business, digital and post-digital transformations shaping the world around us. The journal aims to publish high-quality research that advances understanding of how emerging and established technologies influence socio-cultural structures, economic systems, governance frameworks, and organizational practices — helping managers and policymakers engage thoughtfully and intentionally in decisions that shape these dynamics. A common thread across the journal's focus areas is comparative international research on the role of technology in society and its impact on economic, social, entrepreneurial, management, and innovation processes.

The journal welcomes research employing quantitative, mixed-methods, and theoretical approaches to science and technology, technology forecasting, economic growth, government policy, and ethics. It also includes applied perspectives such as business innovation, international management, technology management, and engineering, as well as development-oriented approaches including technology transfer, technology assessment, and economic leapfrogging.

Additional information on aims and scope for specific topics can be found in the journal's Special Issues and Article Collections.

### Peer Review

- Follows a **double anonymized** review process.
- Initial editor assessment for suitability, then review by a minimum of **two independent reviewers**.
- Acceptance/rejection decisions are made by the editors.
- Editors are **not involved** in decisions on papers that:
  - they wrote themselves;
  - were written by family members or colleagues;
  - relate to products or services in which they have an interest.
- Such submissions follow usual procedures, handled independently of the involved editor and their research group.
- **Appeals:** One formal appeal per submission is allowed, following Elsevier's Appeal Policy; the appeal decision is final.

**Special issues and article collections:** Same review process applies. A guest editor may send submissions to reviewers and recommend a decision, but the journal editor oversees the process and makes the final decision.

## Ethics and Policies

### Ethics in Publishing

Authors must follow Elsevier's Publishing Ethics Policy.

### Submission Declaration

By submitting, authors confirm that:
- The work has not been published previously (except as a preprint, abstract, published lecture, academic thesis, or registered report).
- The article is not under consideration elsewhere.
- Publication is approved by all authors and (tacitly or explicitly) by the responsible authorities where the work was carried out.
- If accepted, it will not be published elsewhere in the same form (any language, including electronically) without written consent of the copyright holder.
- Manuscripts may be checked with screening tools for policy compliance.

### Authorship

All authors must have contributed substantially to:
1. Conception/design of the study, or acquisition, analysis, or interpretation of data.
2. Drafting or critically revising the article.
3. Final approval of the version submitted.

All authors are accountable for the accuracy and integrity of the work. A **single corresponding author** must be appointed to handle all communication; only the corresponding author's affiliation determines APC/discount eligibility.

### Changes to Authorship

- Authorship changes are generally **not permitted after submission**, and **never after acceptance**.
- Before acceptance, changes require:
  - An **Authorship Change Request form**, submitted by the corresponding author;
  - A stated reason;
  - Written confirmation from **all** authors (including those added/removed).
- Review may pause while a request is considered.
- Non-compliant requests can lead to rejection or, post-publication, retraction.

### Declaration of Competing Interests

All authors must disclose financial/personal relationships that could bias their work (employment, consultancies, stock ownership, honoraria, paid expert testimony, patents, grants/funding, editorial board affiliation, etc.).

- The declarations tool must always be completed ("I have nothing to declare" if none apply).
- Authors with a journal role must add a specific disclosure statement under "Other Activities" and inform the journal/publisher before submitting.
- The resulting declaration must be uploaded as a **.doc/.docx** file (no signatures required).

### Funding Sources

- Disclose all funding sources and the sponsor's role (or lack thereof) in study design, data collection/analysis, writing, and the decision to submit.
- Standard format:
  > "Funding: This work was supported by [Funder] [grant numbers]..."
- If no funding: state
  > "This research did not receive any specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors."

### Declaration of Generative AI Use

- Use of generative AI/AI-assisted tools must be **declared at submission**.
- AI tools **may support** manuscript preparation but must never substitute for human critical thinking, expertise, and evaluation; human oversight is required.
- Authors remain fully responsible for:
  - Verifying accuracy, completeness, and impartiality of AI output (including checking for fabricated references);
  - Editing all material so the manuscript reflects the authors' own analysis and ideas;
  - Ensuring accuracy/originality of AI-generated images with attribution;
  - Transparency toward readers about tool/source use;
  - Data privacy and IP compliance when using AI tools.
- **AI tools cannot be listed as authors/co-authors.**
- If used, add a disclosure section titled **"Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process"** placed **before the reference list**, e.g.:
  > "During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [TOOL/SERVICE] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the published article."
- Declaration **not required** for basic tools (grammar/spell-check/reference tools) or accessibility-only assistive tech.
- If nothing to disclose, no statement is needed.
- Reviewers/editors may **not** upload unpublished manuscripts into generative AI tools, though AI may assist with review-report language or literature searches.

### Preprints

- Preprint sharing (e.g., preprint servers) does **not** count as prior publication.
- The journal offers **free preprint posting on SSRN** during submission, available publicly once the initial desk review passes.
- Benefits: early DOI registration, link to the version of record, early citation/collaboration opportunities.
- Posting/not posting a preprint does not affect the editorial outcome.
- Corresponding author must get approval from all co-authors before public preprint posting.

### Use of Inclusive Language

Avoid content implying superiority based on age, gender, race, ethnicity, culture, sexual orientation, or disability/health condition. Prefer gender-neutral plural nouns over "he/she." Avoid slang, stereotypes, and unwarranted assumptions about readers' beliefs.

### Reporting Sex- and Gender-Based Analyses

- No single universal standard exists; integrate sex/gender-based analysis (SGBA) into research design where relevant (human, animal, or eukaryotic cell research).
- Explicitly define how "sex" and "gender" are used, per the **SAGER guidelines/checklist**.
- **Sex**: biological attributes (chromosomes, hormones, anatomy); binary categorization assigned at birth, though intersex/DSD variation exists.
- **Gender**: socially constructed roles/identities/behaviors, culturally and historically variable.
- Papers should clearly define usage where relevant; the journal does not dictate author beliefs.

### Jurisdictional Claims

- Elsevier maintains neutrality on territorial/jurisdictional disputes.
- **Maps**: show only the studied area (not a larger bounding region) and include the disclaimer that map lines don't necessarily depict accepted national boundaries.
- **Institutional affiliations**: use full standard title or standard abbreviation for verifiability.

### Delayed Publication

Contact the editorial office / Journal Article Publishing Support Center as early as possible if delay is needed; delay cannot be guaranteed beyond the agreed period.

### Withdrawal, Correction or Retraction

See Elsevier's Article Correction, Retraction and Removal Policy.

## Writing and Formatting

### File Format

- Provide **editable source files** for the entire submission (figures, tables, text). PDF is **not** an acceptable source file.
- Word files: **.doc/.docx**, single-column layout (double-column only for LaTeX).
- LaTeX files: **.tex**.
- Remove strikethrough/underline unless scientifically significant.
- Run spell-check/grammar-check.

### LaTeX

Use of Elsevier's LaTeX template is encouraged. Editable source files required at submission/revision. Support: LaTeX submission instructions/templates, JAPSC LaTeX FAQs, Researcher Academy's LaTeX guide.

### Title Page

Must include:
- **Article title** — concise, informative, avoid abbreviations/formulae unless well established (e.g., DNA).
- **Author names** — full given/family names, matching submission-system order; verify spelling.
- **Affiliations** — below author names, linked via lower-case superscript letters; full postal address + country + email if available.
- **Corresponding author** — clearly indicated, with up-to-date contact info for all stages (including post-publication).
- **Present/permanent address** (if changed since the work) — indicated via footnote with superscript Arabic numerals; original affiliation remains the main one.

### Double Anonymized Peer Review

- Author identities are concealed from reviewers and vice versa.
- Submit **two separate files**:
  1. **Title page** (with author details, acknowledgements, competing interest declaration if not filed separately, corresponding author's full address/email).
  2. **Anonymized manuscript** — main text, references, tables; must contain **no identifying information** (names, affiliations, acknowledgements).

### Abstract

- Concise, factual, **max 250 words**.
- States purpose, principal results, major conclusions.
- Must stand alone (often shown separately from the article).
- Avoid references (cite author/year if essential).
- Define non-standard abbreviations at first use.

### Keywords

- **1–7 keywords**, in English.
- Avoid multi-word keywords (e.g., using "and"/"of").
- Use abbreviations only if firmly established in the field.

### Highlights

- Encouraged (not mandatory).
- **3–5 bullet points**, each **max 85 characters** (incl. spaces), capturing novel results/methods.
- Submit as a **separate editable file** with "highlights" in the filename.

### Tables

- Submit as **editable text**, not images.
- Place near relevant text or on separate page(s) at the end.
- Cite all tables in-text; number consecutively; include captions.
- Notes go below the table body.
- Avoid vertical rules/shading in cells; avoid duplicating data shown elsewhere.

### Figures, Images and Artwork

- Submit as **separate files**, following Elsevier's artwork/media instructions.
- Cite all images in-text; number in order of appearance; use logical file naming (e.g., Figure_1, Figure_2).
- Text graphics may be embedded (also in LaTeX source).
- **Captions**: brief title + description; minimize on-image text; explain symbols/abbreviations.

**Format/resolution requirements:**
| Type | Format | Min. Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Vector drawings | EPS or PDF (embed font or convert text to graphics) | — |
| Color/grayscale photographs (halftones) | TIFF, JPG, PNG | 300 dpi (single column ≥1063 px; full page ≥2244 px) |
| Bitmapped line drawings | TIFF, JPG, PNG | 1000 dpi (single column ≥3543 px; full page ≥7480 px) |
| Combination bitmap line/halftone (color/grayscale) | TIFF, JPG, PNG | 500 dpi (single column ≥1772 px; full page ≥3740 px) |

**Avoid:** low-resolution files, disproportionately small text, multiple images/graphs merged into one (hurts accessibility).

**Color artwork:** published in color online at no extra step; ensure accessibility for color-vision-impaired readers.

### Generative AI and Figures, Images and Other Artwork

- AI tools **may** be used for explanatory images (flow charts, conceptual diagrams, schematics) and for data visualizations **directly derived from underlying data** via reproducible methods.
- AI tools **must not** be used to create/alter images representing primary observed/experimental data (e.g., western blots, microscopy, histology, patient images) not directly obtained in the research — including brightness/contrast/color adjustments (use standard image-processing software instead).
- AI-assisted research methods (e.g., AI imaging for data collection/interpretation, predictive modelling) remain permitted.
- Disclose AI-generated images in **each image caption** and in the general AI disclosure statement.

### Supplementary Material

- Encouraged (applications, images, sound clips, etc.).
- Must be accurate/relevant, cited in-text, submitted together with the manuscript, with a descriptive caption each.
- Post-submission, can only be added/replaced during **revision**.
- Published exactly as received (not typeset/formatted by production).

### Video

- Accepted to support research; include links/notes in text at the placement point.
- Clear, content-related file names.
- Preferred max size: **150 MB/file, 1 GB total**.
- Provide a "still" image per file.
- Include descriptive text for accessibility (important for print too, since video can't be embedded there).

### Research Data

- Research data = results of observations/experimentation validating findings (may include software, code, models, algorithms, protocols, methods, etc.).
- This journal follows **Option C**:
  1. Deposit data in a relevant repository.
  2. Cite and link the dataset in the article.
  3. If not possible, provide a statement explaining why.

### Data Statement

- Required at submission — state data availability (or reason for unavailability, e.g., sensitive/confidential data). Appears with the published article.

### Data Linking

- Link the article to the dataset via: the submission-system prompt, automatic repository banners (for supported repositories), or in-text identifiers (format: `Database: 12345`, e.g., `TAIR: AT1G01020`).

### Co-submission of Related Data, Methods or Protocols

- May co-submit a related data/methods/protocols paper to **Data in Brief** or **MethodsX** at the same time as the main manuscript.
- Use their mandatory templates; submit via the "Attach files" step in Editorial Manager.
- Personalized APC for the co-submission is provided only after acceptance.

### Article Structure

**Sections**
- Numbered sections/subsections (1.1, 1.1.1, 1.1.2, 1.2, ...); use numbers when cross-referencing (not "the text").
- Optional brief subsection headings on their own line.
- Abstract is not part of section numbering.

**Footnotes**
- Use sparingly; number consecutively (auto-generated or listed in a separate end section).

**Author Contributions (CRediT)**
Corresponding authors must assign CRediT roles among:
Conceptualization · Data curation · Formal analysis · Funding acquisition · Investigation · Methodology · Project administration · Resources · Software · Supervision · Validation · Visualization · Writing – original draft · Writing – review and editing

(Not all roles apply to every paper; authors may hold multiple roles.)

**Appendices**
- Label individually (A, B, ...).
- Equations: `Eq. (A.1)`, `Eq. (A.2)`, then `Eq. (B.1)` etc. for subsequent appendices.
- Same pattern for tables/figures: `Table A.1`, `Fig. A.1`, etc.

**Vitae**
- Short bio per author, **max 100 words**, editable format (e.g., Word, not PDF).

### References

**In-text**
- Every in-text citation must appear in the reference list and vice versa.
- Abstract references must be given in full.
- Avoid unpublished results/personal communications in the reference list (mention in-text instead); if included, replace the date with "unpublished results" or "personal communication."
- "In press" implies acceptance for publication.
- DOIs encouraged as reference links.
- Verify all reference data before submission (correct names, titles, years, pagination) to enable indexing links (Scopus, Crossref, PubMed).

**Format**
- No strict format required at submission, but must be **consistent** throughout.
- Must include (where applicable): author names, journal/book titles, chapter/article titles, publication year, volume, article number or pagination.
- Elsevier's house style is applied after acceptance, at proof stage.

**Style**
- Citation style: **APA** (Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association, 7th ed., 2020, ISBN 978-1-4338-3215-4).
- Reference list: alphabetical, then chronological; multiple works by the same author(s)/year get suffixes (a, b, c...).

**Examples**

- *Journal article:*
  > Van der Geer, J., Handgraaf T., & Lupton, R. A. (2020). The art of writing a scientific article. *Journal of Scientific Communications, 163*, 51–59. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sc.2020.00372

- *Journal article with article number:*
  > Van der Geer, J., Handgraaf, T., & Lupton, R. A. (2022). The art of writing a scientific article. *Heliyon, 19*, Article e00205. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e00205

- *Book:*
  > Strunk, W., Jr., & White, E. B. (2000). *The elements of style* (4th ed.). Longman (Chapter 4).

- *Book chapter:*
  > Mettam, G. R., & Adams, L. B. (2020). How to prepare an electronic version of your article. In B. S. Jones & R. Z. Smith (Eds.), *Introduction to the electronic age* (pp. 281–304). E-Publishing Inc.

- *Website:*
  > Powertech Systems. (2022). *Lithium-ion vs lead-acid cost analysis.* Retrieved from http://www.powertechsystems.eu/home/tech-corner/lithium-ion-vs-lead-acid-cost-analysis/. Accessed January 6, 2022.

- *Dataset:*
  > Oguro, M., Imahiro, S., Saito, S., & Nakashizuka, T. (2015). *Mortality data for Japanese oak wilt disease and surrounding forest compositions* [dataset]. Mendeley Data, v1. https://doi.org/10.17632/xwj98nb39r.1

- *Conference paper/poster:*
  > Engle, E. K., Cash, T. F., & Jarry, J. L. (2019, November). *The Body Image Behaviours Inventory-3: Development and validation of the Body Image Compulsive Actions and Body Image Avoidance Scales.* Poster session presentation at the meeting of the Association for Behavioural and Cognitive Therapies, New York, NY.

- *Software:*
  > Coon, E., Berndt, M., Jan, A., Svyatsky, D., Atchley, A., Kikinzon, E., Harp, D., Manzini, G., Shelef, E., Lipnikov, K., Garimella, R., Xu, C., Moulton, D., Karra, S., Painter, S., Jafarov, E., & Molins, S. (2020). *Advanced Terrestrial Simulator (ATS)* (Version 0.88) [Computer software]. Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3727209

**Web references**
- Provide full URL + access date (plus DOI/author/date if known); list separately after the reference list, or integrate into it.

**Data references**
- Include: author name(s), dataset title, repository, version, year, global persistent identifier.
- Precede with `[dataset]` (this tag won't appear in the published version).

**Preprint references**
- Clearly mark as "preprint" or name the preprint server; include the preprint DOI.
- If a formal peer-reviewed version later becomes available, cite that instead.

**Reference management**
- CSL-compatible tools (e.g., Mendeley Reference Manager) supported.
- Remove all field codes before final submission if a citation plug-in was used.

## Submitting Your Manuscript

### Submission Checklist

- [ ] Corresponding author designated with full contact details (email, postal address, phone).
- [ ] All files uploaded (artwork, videos, supplementary materials, tables, footnotes, captions).
- [ ] Spelling/grammar checked.
- [ ] All in-text references match the reference list (and vice versa).
- [ ] Permissions obtained for any copyrighted material used (including web sources).
- [ ] For OA articles: all authors aware of APC responsibility (institution/funder may cover it).

### Submit Online

- Submission system converts files to a single PDF for peer review.
- **Editable files** (Word/LaTeX) are required for final typesetting.
- All correspondence (decisions, revision requests) sent via email.

### After Receiving a Final Decision

**Article Transfer Service**
- If deemed more suitable for another Elsevier journal, authors may be invited to transfer via the Article Transfer Service (recommendation from editor, managing editor, tool, or combination).
- If accepted, the manuscript is independently reviewed at the new journal, with a chance for revisions.

### Publishing Agreement

- Completed after acceptance; corresponding author receives an online agreement link by email.

### License Options

- Authors choose an open access license option, determining reuse rights; review options and any funder requirements beforehand.

### Permission for Copyrighted Works

- Written permission required for excerpts from other copyrighted works, with source credit, using Elsevier's permission request/license form.

### Proof Correction

- Proof corrections requested within **2 days** of receipt.
- Online proofing system (Word-like) allows annotation, comments, and query responses; PDF annotation also accepted.
- Alt text/audio descriptions may be AI-generated by Elsevier — authors should verify these in proofs.
- Purpose: check typesetting, editing, completeness, correctness — not substantive changes (those require editor approval).

### Share Link

- Corresponding author receives a **Share Link** (50 days free access) to share the final published article.
- Not provided for open access articles (which are openly available via the DOI).

### Responsible Sharing

- Authors are encouraged to share/promote their article via appropriate channels for visibility and scientific exchange.
